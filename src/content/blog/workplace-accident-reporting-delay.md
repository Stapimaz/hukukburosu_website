## Employer's Guide: What to Do If the Workplace Accident Reporting Period is Missed?

No matter how strict occupational health and safety measures are taken in workplaces, the risk of occupational accidents is always present. When an accident occurs, the process is highly stressful for employers and human resources managers. The most common mistake made in these moments of crisis is missing the extremely strict 3-working-day notification period specified in the legislation due to panic or lack of information. In some cases, the worker hides the accident from the employer, and by the time hospital records come to you days later, the period has long passed.

**Don't Panic, Legal Solutions Are Available!**

Having missed the workplace accident notification period does not mean that your company is left completely defenseless or will definitively face massive compensation claims. Our legal system grants employers who act in good faith and manage the process correctly the opportunity to minimize risks. In this article, we discussed the legal risks employers will face in delayed reporting of workplace accidents and strategic solutions that will enable you to emerge from this situation with minimal damage.

### 1. What Are the Risks Waiting for the Employer When the Notification Period is Missed?

In accordance with Law No. 5510, it is mandatory to notify SGK within a maximum of 3 working days from the date the workplace accident occurs (or from the date it is learned in accidents outside the workplace). If this period is exceeded, your company faces these three major risks:

* **Administrative Fine:** For every notification not made on time, an administrative fine is issued at rates varying according to your company size and hazard class.
* **SGK's Recourse Risk (The Most Dangerous Stage):** Temporary incapacity allowances (report money), hospital expenses, and treatment costs paid to the worker by SGK during the period the notification was delayed are requested back from the employer down to the last penny.
* **Presumption of Fault:** Delayed or absent notification can create the perception that the employer "tried to hide the accident" in a possible compensation lawsuit, putting the company at a disadvantage before the court.

### 2. Legal Solutions the Employer Can Apply in Case of Delay

If you missed the deadline or learned about the accident late, strategic steps you can take to protect the company's financial and legal existence are as follows:

**Documenting Justifiable Grounds (Force Majeure and Late Learning)**
If the accident occurred outside the workplace (for example, on an official business trip) and the worker did not inform you on time, the 3-day period starts not on the day of the accident, but on the day you officially learned about it. A late-dated WhatsApp message, email, or health report registration date sent by the worker to you is considered the "learning day". Retaining these evidences saves you from SGK's recourse lawsuits.

**Making an Immediate Notification, Even If Delayed**
The thought "The deadline passed anyway, let's not notify at all" can result in disaster for the company. For every day the notification is not made, SGK's treatment costs increase, and the recourse amount grows. Even if the deadline has passed, a notification must be made immediately via e-SGK, and the reason for the delay must be annotated in legal language in the explanation section.

**Examining Hospital Records and the Objection Process**
Sometimes, a worker may portray an accident they had at home over the weekend as "happened at work", or vice versa. The worker's statement in the epicrisis report kept by the doctor in the emergency room records of the first hospital applied to is of vital importance. If the worker initially said "I fell at home" and later turned this into a workplace accident, these records that the employer will present to SGK and the court prove the company's flawlessness.
